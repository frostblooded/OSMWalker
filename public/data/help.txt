# ℹ Getting started
## What OSM Walker is {about}
OSM Walker is a little life you watch, not steer. A small character walks the real streets of a real city, taken from OpenStreetMap. He picks places to visit, walks there, does something at each one, and slowly gets stronger, richer and more experienced.

You don't control him. Everything he does is decided by the rules on these pages: where he goes, what he does there, what he buys, and when he travels to another city or country. Your part is to check in, see where he has got to, and read what he has been up to. Now and then you can also do something small for him, such as sending a care package (see Little touches). It is always optional: he lives just as well if you never do. He lives on while the game is closed too (see While you were away).

Each page in this Help explains one part of the game in detail, with the real numbers the game uses.
## Choosing a city {city_search}
On the first launch you pick where he starts. Start typing the name of a town or city. After {SEARCH_MIN_CHARS} letters and a short pause, suggestions appear by themselves; you can also tap 🔍 or press Enter to search right away. Only towns, cities, villages and suburbs are suggested, and missing accents or small typos are fine ("compiegne" finds Compiegne). Tap a suggestion to start there.

If the search servers can't be reached, it says "Couldn't reach the search server": check your connection and try again. "Nothing found" means there really is no such place. Answers are remembered until you close the game, so searching the same name again is instant.

The game then downloads the streets around that spot. He starts on the street corner nearest to the result and stays inside that city until he travels somewhere else (see Trips to other cities).

The area of the result decides the city box he picks goals in. It is grown or shrunk so that it reaches at least {MIN_CITY_KM} km and at most {MAX_CITY_KM} km from the centre, so a tiny village still has room to walk and a whole country never counts as one city.
## Real time, and time away {offline}
While the game is open on screen he lives in real time: one minute on your clock is one minute for him. A single step never counts for more than {MAX_STEP_S} second, so a stall can't make him jump ahead.

When you close the game or hide the tab, the time doesn't stop for him. When you come back, the time you were away passes for him in one go, and a "While you were away" card tells you what he did (see While you were away).

The game saves every {SAVE_EVERY_S} seconds and when you leave, on this device only. Clearing the browser's site data starts you over. If a save can't be read, the game says so and starts over, and keeps a copy of the old save on the device in case it can be fixed.
## While you were away {away}
When the game opens again after at least {MIN_AWAY_MIN} minute away (closed, or the tab hidden), that time passes for him at once, at full speed, the same as if you had watched. Only the first {MAX_AWAY_H} hours after you left count, so he lives through at most {MAX_AWAY_H} hours of a longer absence; the card says how much was left over.

He doesn't walk the map while you are away, so no streets have to load. Instead he hops from place to place by the usual rules:
- he picks each place like any goal (see Picking the next goal), from the places already loaded in his city, and walks the straight line to it times {ROAD_FACTOR}, at his usual speed,
- he does one action there and stays {STAY_MIN} minute (see What he does at places),
- his meters, buffs and money change as they always do, so he can get hungry, earn, buy gear, level up, and faint and wake in hospital,
- he takes on quests and joins world events as usual (see Quests),
- he rolls the trip dice as usual and pays for the trip (see Trips to other cities), but not while he is on a quest. A trip that wouldn't be over before you come back doesn't start: he stays, and the trip dice roll again at his next stop.

While you are away day and night still follow the real sun and the season counts, and closed places stay closed (when every place near enough is closed he walks to a random spot instead), but the weather doesn't: the catch-up lives through those hours with no weather at all, so it can't rain on him while you're gone (see Weather).

In a city whose streets haven't loaded (one he reached while you were away), he visits made-up places of the usual kinds, such as "a cafe in Plovdiv". They have the actions of real places of that kind, but give no first-visit XP, cards, souvenirs, Placedex entries, crawl progress or crawl perks. He leaves such a city from its bus station or a gas station in the middle, on his bike to a near town, hitchhiking when broke (see Other ways to travel), or on a long walk (see Long walks).

If he was on any ride to another city when you left, the ride goes on first. If it isn't over by the time you are back (a long walk, say), he is still on his way when you return. On reopen the game waits up to {CATCHUP_WAIT_S} seconds for the streets around him to come out of the device's cache, so he visits the real places nearby.

At the end he stands at the last place he visited, on the nearest street corner within {SNAP_M} m, or there on his own until the streets around it load, and picks his next goal. Everything he did is in his History at the time it happened, with a marker for each place but no trail, because he didn't really walk the streets in between.

The card shows how long you were away, how many places he visited and how many were new, how far he walked, the cities he reached, his money and level before and after, and how many things he bought. If the reopen earned a clover (see Clovers and care packages), it says so, and now and then it holds one small choice (see One choice on reopen). Tap "events, newest first" to see everything he did. "Carry on" or ✕ Close closes it.
## His week {recap}
The game keeps a tally of his week: how far he walked, how many places he stopped at and how many were new, the kind of place he stopped at most, the cities he arrived in, and his money and level when the week began. A week starts on Monday at midnight on your phone's clock.

After a week ends, the next While you were away sheet opens with two cards about it, His week and His week's progress, ready for a screenshot. Each week's recap shows once; a week with no stops has none.
## Daily forecast {forecast}
Every day has one forecast, shown on the top card under his goal. It is picked from the date on your phone, so everyone playing on the same date gets the same one, and it lasts until midnight. He doesn't plan around it, but it changes how some things go that day, and so what he can afford:
{FORECAST_LIST}

While you are away, each day he lives through has its own forecast.
# 🚶 Walking
## How he walks {walking}
On foot he walks at {WALK_KMH} km/h, about {WALK_M_PER_MIN} m a minute, along real streets, paths and footways. Motorways and other roads you can't walk on are left out. A bike or a motor vehicle makes him faster (see Speed and vehicles).

He always walks towards a goal (see Places of note and His goal). He plans the shortest route over the streets that are loaded so far, and plans again as more streets load. If the streets ahead of him are not downloaded yet, he stands at the edge and "Loading streets…" shows at the top until they arrive.

If he has walked more than 2.5 times the straight-line distance to his goal plus 2 km and still isn't there, the goal is probably across water or behind a fence, so he gives up on it and picks another. After {MAX_SKIPS} give-ups in a row he just walks down a random street for a while.
## Speed and vehicles {speed}
His speed depends on how he gets about:
- on foot: {WALK_KMH} km/h,
- bike: {BIKE_KMH} km/h,
- scooter or motorbike: {MOTORCYCLE_KMH} km/h,
- car: {CITY_CAR_KMH} km/h (city traffic),
- a fantasy mount: see Fantasy mounts.

He rides whatever he wears in his vehicle slot. Bike shops sell bikes, motorcycle dealers scooters and motorbikes, and car dealers cars. A car always beats a motorbike, and a motorbike a bike, whatever their material. A vehicle's material adds {SPEED_PER_POWER}% speed per point of power, and its modifiers don't add speed.

Every level above 1 adds {SPEED_PER_LEVEL_PCT} speed. When his energy is under {TIRED_ENERGY} he slows down, to half speed at 0 energy. In snow he goes at {SNOW_SPEED_PCT} of his speed on foot or by bike (see Weather).

Riding is less tiring than walking: a bike takes {BIKE_EFFORT_PCT} of the energy per km, and a motorbike or car {MOTOR_EFFORT_PCT}. The goal line's time ("12 min") uses his speed right now.
## Fantasy mounts {mounts}
Now and then the real map has a little magic in it. These mounts can be had, each from a real place of the right kind:
- a giant tortoise from a pet shop: {TORTOISE_KMH} km/h, and he never tires on it,
- a broomstick from a hardware or DIY shop: {BROOM_KMH} km/h,
- a flying carpet from a carpet shop: {CARPET_KMH} km/h,
- a dragon from the stable at a real castle: {DRAGON_KMH} km/h.

The tortoise, broomstick and carpet sit beside the bike, scooter and car: each ranks with that vehicle, so he swaps one for the other only when the new one is stronger (a better material). A pet, hardware or carpet shop only has one in {MOUNT_STOCK_PCT} of the time he asks. The dragon is the top tier, above any car: from {CHEAP_DRAGON}, it is what he saves up for once he has a car (see Saving for a vehicle).

Flyers (the broomstick, the carpet and the dragon) don't follow the streets. When his goal is at least {FLY_MIN_M} m away and a park or garden is loaded near it (at most {LAND_MAX_M} m from the goal, and at most {LAND_SHARE_PCT} as far from it as he is), he flies there in a straight line, lands, and walks the rest on foot, at walking speed. Otherwise he goes by the streets like anyone else. While you're away the same rules decide when he flies, except that any such park will do, even one far from a street. On the map his mount shows at his side, except a sulking dragon, which stays behind.

A dragon eats {DRAGON_UPKEEP} of gold a day. He pays it first thing each day, before he moves on (also while you're away); if he hasn't got it, the dragon sulks and won't fly (if it starts to sulk in the air, he comes down at the nearest corner), and he walks until he can pay. Nothing is lost. On a day it is fed it brings back treasure {DRAGON_TREASURE_PCT} of the time, worth {DRAGON_TREASURE_MIN} to {DRAGON_TREASURE_MAX}. Riding a broomstick takes {BROOM_EFFORT_PCT} of the walking energy per km, a carpet {CARPET_EFFORT_PCT} and a dragon {DRAGON_EFFORT_PCT}.
## Place creatures {creatures}
A little folklore lives in the city. When he reaches a place where a creature lives (a real one or one the game made up, such as an old well), it shows up {CREATURE_PCT} of the time, but he meets at most one creature a day (also while you're away). Each one offers him a choice:
{CREATURE_LIST}

The troll's riddle works {RIDDLE_PCT} of the time, plus {RIDDLE_PER_WIT_PCT} for each point of Wits above 1, up to {RIDDLE_CAP_PCT}. The cleverer he is, the more often he tries it on his own. He only pays what he has. For now he always makes his own pick; soon you'll be able to tap one before a timer runs out. Each creature he meets gets a tile in the Collection (Places tab).
## Ley lines {ley_lines}
Old lines of power run between the real churches and other places of worship, monuments, memorials and castles of his city: each one is joined to the nearest other one the game has loaded. Open the Stats sheet to see them on the map as dotted indigo lines.

Going within {LEY_NEAR_M} m of a line, on foot or riding (but not on a trip to another city), fills his Magic by {LEY_PER_KM} for every km, and charms him: {LEY_CHARM_LUCK} luck for {LEY_CHARM_MIN} minutes after he last went along one. Reaching any church, monument, memorial or castle gives {LEY_BURST} Magic at once, once a day for each place, as each is a point where the lines meet. While you're away there is no map to walk along, but the real places he reaches still give their burst (the made-up ones of a city with no places loaded don't).
## Spells {spells}
Old books in real libraries and bookshops hide little spells. Each time he reaches one there is a {LEARN_PCT} chance he learns a spell he doesn't know yet (also while you're away, but not at a made-up library). He casts them himself, at most one at a time, paying in Magic:
{SPELL_LIST}

A spell that tops up a meter is cast as soon as that meter is under {SPELL_AT}. A spell that gives a buff is cast only when he has at least {SPARE_MAGIC} Magic and the buff isn't on, so some Magic is always left for the others. The Stats sheet lists the spells he knows and how often he cast each, and each one gets a tile in the Collection (Gear tab).
## The shop after midnight {midnight_shop}
In the small hours ({TIME_MIDNIGHT}) some real shops open for those who knock: {MIDNIGHT_SHOPS}. When he knocks, {ENCHANTED_PCT} of the time the shelf holds an enchanted item, and otherwise a trinket.

An enchanted item is ordinary gear (a weapon, clothes or a trinket) with one enchantment on top of at most {ENCHANTED_MODS_MAX} other modifiers, so it is never Common: {ENCHANT_LIST}. It costs {ENCHANTED_PRICE}× the usual price plus {ENCHANTED_MAGIC} Magic, and he buys it only if it is an upgrade, like any gear. Magic from enchanted gear fills his Magic meter for every km he walks, shown on the Stats sheet.

A trinket is used on the spot:
{TRINKET_LIST}

He pays only when he has the euros (keeping his reserve) and the Magic. Everything he finds there gets a tile in the Collection (Gear tab, Midnight finds).
## Places of note {places}
His goals are named places from the map: shops, cafes, restaurants, parks, museums, churches, stations, bridges and many more. A street bridge is named after its street ("Bridge on Main Street") and an unnamed footbridge is just "Footbridge"; bridges rank low, so he heads for one only now and then. Only places inside the current city box count, and places closer than {ARRIVE_M} m to him are skipped so he always really walks somewhere.

Each place has a rank from the map, where 1 is the most important in its area, so big sights and busy shops come up more often than small ones. How far away a place is and which way it lies count too (see Picking the next goal).

If no places have loaded yet, he heads for a random spot in the city box instead and walks on through it without stopping.

He counts as arrived when he is within {ARRIVE_M} m of the place.
## Made-up places {made_places}
The real map comes first, but a city sometimes lacks what he needs, and quests need somewhere to happen. So once the streets around him have loaded in a new city, the game adds a few places of its own. They show on the map with the game's own round icons (see Map icons) and work like any other place: he picks them as goals and does their actions. They give no cards, souvenirs, Placedex entries or crawl progress, since those are for real places, but the first of each extra kind and his first wizard's tower count as odd places in the Collection.

Must-haves fill gaps. If none of the loaded places is one of these, the city gets one near its centre, on a street:
- a bus station ("Plovdiv coach station"), so he can leave,
- a gas station, so a car can refuel,
- somewhere to eat (an action that fills him by at least {MEAL_FULLNESS}),
- somewhere to sleep (an action that gives at least {BED_ENERGY} energy),
- a job centre, so he can always earn.

A city of {AIRPORT_POP} people or more with no real airport within {AIRPORT_KM} km gets an airport of its own outside it, so planes can fly there too.

Extras give stories a stage: notice boards, an adventurers' hall, old wells, lookouts, hidden shrines and storytellers' corners, each next to a fitting real spot (a well in a park, a lookout at a viewpoint), or near the centre when the city has no such spot. How many a city gets depends on its size:
- a town (up to {TOWN_POP} people): {EXTRAS_TOWN},
- a city (up to {CITY_POP} people): {EXTRAS_CITY} and {TOWERS_CITY} wizard's tower,
- a big city: {EXTRAS_CAPITAL} and {TOWERS_CAPITAL} wizard's towers ({TOWERS_MEGA} above {MEGA_POP} people).

Wizard's towers rise at the grandest real landmarks (castles first, then viewpoints, sights, monuments and museums), at least {TOWER_APART_KM} km apart. A city with no landmark gets none.

A city keeps its made-up places: they are rolled from the city's name and saved, so the tower is still at the same castle when he comes back. The save keeps them for the {MADE_CITIES} cities he was in most recently.
## Map icons {map_icons}
The game marks its own places on the map with round icons: blue for travel (bus stations, airports, gas stations), green for food and beds, amber for work, purple for story places, quests and world events (see Quests and World events), indigo for magic, and gold plaques for the monuments he built (see Golden bricks). Real bus stations and the airports he can fly from get one too. Icons show once a whole city fits in view, and their names show from street level. At most the {ICON_CAP} nearest places to him show at once (his monuments always show), with up to {QUEST_ICON_CAP} quests and {EVENT_ICON_CAP} events, and none while he rides to another city.
## Picking the next goal {goal_pick}
He picks his next goal like someone with a plan for the day: not always the nearest place, not across town on a whim, and mostly carrying on the way he was going. Every place in the city box gets a weight, and the bigger its weight the likelier it is picked. The weight is four numbers multiplied together:

Rank: 1 / rank, so a rank 1 place weighs 1 and a rank 4 place weighs 0.25.

Distance: places about {GOAL_PEAK_M} m away weigh most (1). The weight falls off smoothly both ways: a place {NEAR_M} m away weighs {NEAR_PCT} as much, one {FAR_KM} km away {FAR_PCT}, so he hops between nearby spots and only now and then crosses the city.

Heading: his heading is the direction from where his last walk started to where it ended. A place straight ahead weighs {HEADING_RATIO} times one straight behind him, with places to the side in between. Right after arriving in a new city he has no heading yet, so every direction counts the same.

Need: a place weighs 1 + {NEED_BOOST} x the need score of the best thing he can do there right now, times how well it suits him (see His needs). So a place that meets an empty need in full weighs {NEED_MAX} times as much, and at half of {NEED_AT} {NEED_HALF} times. When he is hungry he heads for food, when tired for coffee or a bed, and when he is broke or saving for a trip or a vehicle, for paid work. A full bag counts as an empty need met in full by resale shops (see The bag and selling). Once he has saved up for his next vehicle and isn't saving for a fare, a shop that sells it weighs {GOAL_SHOP_PULL} times as much (see Saving for a vehicle). Only an urgent need beats that. A whisper he follows (see Whispers) changes this too: Eat and Rest make those meters feel empty, so they count as rock bottom (see Urgent below), Save counts as saving for a trip, and Explore makes each place he has never been to weigh {EXPLORE_PULL} times as much. His wants and fears (see Wants and fears) multiply a place's need too: ×{WANT_PULL} for each want of its kind, ×{FEAR_PULL} for a fear. So do his emotion, ×{EMO_PULL} for its kinds of place (see Emotions), and his tastes, ×{LIKE_PULL} for a kind he loves and ×{DISLIKE_PULL} for one he can't stand (see Hidden tastes).

Urgent: while a meter is under {URGENT_AT} and some open place in the city box can fix it, he only looks at places that fix an urgent need, and rank, the {GOAL_PEAK_M} m bump and heading drop out: the nearest fix wins. A fix {URGENT_NEAR_M} m away or closer weighs most, one twice as far a quarter as much. He still does as he likes {SLIP_PCT} of the time, unless a meter is under {BOTTOM_AT}: then he always goes, and only places that fix that meter count, even over another urgent need.

The sky multiplies the need number too: a closed place weighs 0 and fixes no urgent need, so he never sets off for a shop at night (see Opening hours), and rain, snow or heat make some places pull more or less (see Weather). If every place near enough is closed he walks to a random spot.

Random spots (while no places are loaded) follow the same distance and heading odds, and always stay inside the city box. Debug shows all four numbers for every pick.
## His goal {goal}
While he walks, the card at the top shows where he is going, how far it is and how long the walk will take, for example "🎯 Going to Central Market · 1.2 km · ~14 min". The time is approximate: it is the planned route at his current speed. A 🎯 pin on the map marks the goal.

Tap that line to see him and his goal on the map together. Tap the line again (or "Back to him" while the camera follows him) to go back to him. The view also goes back to him by itself when he picks a new goal.

While he stays at a place the line reads "Resting at" and the place's name.
## Staying somewhere {countdown}
At each place of note he stops and does one action (see What he does at places). Every stop lasts exactly {STAY_MIN} minute, whatever the action is, so he is never standing still for long.

While he stays, the top card shows "Moving again in 0:59" and an orange bar that fills up as the minute passes. When it is full he picks his next goal and sets off.
## The map {map}
The map is drawn from OpenStreetMap data by OpenFreeMap; the ⓘ at the bottom left shows the credits when you tap it. He is the round orange face (see His face and bubble). A smaller round {RIVAL_ICON} beside him, on a cream badge, is Viktor, for a while after they meet (see Viktor, his rival). The game moves him a few times a second and his face glides smoothly between those steps.

While the camera follows him (the Camera follows him switch in the menu, on at first) the map stays still while he walks around the middle, and glides over to him once he is more than {MIDDLE_PCT}% of the view away from the middle. That way the whole map is only redrawn every so often, which saves battery. Drag the map to look around, then tap "Back to him" to follow him again. With the switch off the map never moves by itself, and there is no "Back to him".

Use the + and − buttons on the right to zoom in and out; the zoom is remembered. Streets are downloaded in squares around him, {STREET_SQUARES} by {STREET_SQUARES} map tiles of about {TILE_KM} km each (smaller far from the equator), and the squares in the direction he is heading load first. Streets he has seen are kept on the device for {CACHE_DAYS} days, so the same city loads faster next time and works with a weak connection.

During a trip to another city a red line shows the way from where he left to where he is going, and the map zooms out to fit it.
## His face and bubble {look}
His face shows how he feels. The first of these that fits wins:
- 🤒 sick: health below {SICK_AT},
- 😩 tired: energy below {TIRED_ENERGY},
- 😖 dirty: hygiene below {DIRTY_AT},
- 😞 down: mood below {LOW_MOOD},
- 😄 happy: mood {HAPPY_MOOD} or more,
- 🙂 fine: anything else.

While he walks, a bubble over his head shows what he needs most: the icon of his lowest meter under {NEED_AT} (🍔 food, 🥤 a drink, 🛏 rest, 🚿 a wash, 💊 health, 🎈 fun). When he needs nothing it shows the kind of place he is going to (☕ a cafe, 🏛 a museum). On the way to a random spot it shows only a need, and on a trip there is no bubble.

While he stays at a place, a pose for what he is doing floats over him instead: 💤 a nap, ☕ a coffee, 🎵 something fun, 💡 learning, 💶 work, 🛍 shopping, and so on, after what the action gives most. It moves for a few seconds when he gets there and again every {POSE_EVERY_S} seconds, and holds still in between to save battery. His pet, if he has one, sits at his side (see His pet).
## City sound {sound}
The 🔊 City sound switch in the menu (off at first) plays a soft loop for where he is: cafe chatter with the odd clinking cup while he stays at a place to eat or drink, birds and leaves in parks and gardens, and a low street hum anywhere else. On a trip it is quiet. The sound is made on your phone, so it downloads nothing; it stops while the game is in the background.
# 🎯 Actions
## What he does at places {actions}
Every kind of place has its own list of actions. At a cafe he might have an espresso or chat with the barista, at a park he might nap under a tree, at a shop he might browse for gear or sell his loot. There are {ACTION_COUNT} actions for {PLACE_KINDS} kinds of places, plus a few general ones for places that don't have their own list yet.

An action can:
- raise or lower his meters (health, energy, fullness, thirst, mood, hygiene),
- raise his attributes (strength, charm, wits, luck), usually by a small step such as 0.1,
- give XP,
- cost money or pay money (jobs pay a fixed amount or a random amount in a range, to the cent, and more as he grows: see Wages),
- give him a buff or debuff for a while (see Buffs and debuffs),
- offer him an item to buy, or sell something from his bag.

Some actions have a risk: a chance that it goes differently, which adds to what it says, for example "Rakia with the salad, one too many". A risk can cost extra money and change his meters, for better or worse.

What he did pops up at the top for a few seconds, and goes into his History. About {JOKE_PCT} of these lines (not buying or selling) get a dry joke on the end, picked for the kind of place: "Looked at the paintings at the National Museum. The museum did not visit back." Lines players suggest may join them in later updates (see Suggest a line).
## How he chooses {choosing}
At each place he first throws out every action he can't do right now (see Requirements). From the rest he picks one at random, but some actions weigh more than others:
- Every action starts with weight 1.
- Browsing for an item: +1 when he has more than {SKINT_X3}.
- Selling his loot: +2.
- Anything that would leave him under {SKINT} weighs half as much.
- A country's own actions get +{FLAVOUR_WEIGHT} (see Country flavour).
- While he saves up for a trip or his next vehicle, spending weighs {SAVING_SPEND_PCT} as much (see Fares and Saving for a vehicle).
- Browsing the shop of the vehicle he has saved up for: +{GOAL_SHOP}.
- A kind deed while he leans kind: +{KIND_DEED_WEIGHT} (see Kind or rascal).

His needs here are the ones he feels: while he follows an Eat or Rest whisper, the meters it names count as empty (see Whispers).

Then his needs take over (see His needs): the weight is multiplied by 1 + {NEED_BOOST} x the action's need score, by how well it suits him, by {EMO_PULL} when it trains or needs his emotion's attribute (see Emotions), and cut for every need it drains. With a meter under {URGENT_AT}, he only picks from actions that fix an urgent need, but for {SLIP_PCT} of the time; under {BOTTOM_AT}, always, and only from actions that fix that meter.

An action with weight 4 is four times as likely as one with weight 1.
## His needs {urges}
His needs come first, like real ones: a little hungry he may still visit a museum, starving he goes straight for food.

Urge: each meter (fullness, thirst, energy, health, mood, hygiene) is a need below {NEED_AT}. Its urge is the square of how far below it is: 0 at {NEED_AT}, {URGE_HALF} at {NEED_HALF_AT}, 1 at 0. So it grows slowly at first and steeply near empty. Money is a need too: below {SKINT} its urge grows the same way, down to 1 at €0, and while he saves up for a trip or a vehicle it is at least {SAVING_URGE}.

Need score: an action scores the urge of each meter it raises, times how much of the need it meets: a raise of {FULL_FIX} or more meets it in full, a smaller one in part. Paid work scores the money urge, and with a full bag a resale shop's sale scores 1. The scores of all needs an action meets add up, so a meal with a drink beats a meal alone when he is both hungry and thirsty.

Urgent: under {URGENT_AT} a meter is urgent. Under {BOTTOM_AT} it is at rock bottom, and fixing it beats every other need. While any meter is urgent he skips the trip dice and stays in town. A meter an Eat or Rest whisper names feels empty while he follows it, so it counts as rock bottom (see Whispers). See Picking the next goal and How he chooses.

The feed says when a strong need picks his goal, for example "Hungry, heading for Bakery Nord": an urgent need, or one at half of {NEED_AT} or lower. Money only counts there while he has less than {SKINT}, not while he just saves up.

Drain: an action that lowers a meter he needs weighs (1 - that meter's urge) as much, at least {HARM_FLOOR_PCT}, so he doesn't go jogging when he is worn out.

His strengths: an action that trains or needs one of his attributes weighs (that attribute / his average attribute) as much, from 1 up to {STRENGTH_MAX} times, so a strong walker leans to the gym and a charming one to people. A place weighs as much as its best-suited action.
## Requirements {needs}
An action can only be picked when he meets all of its requirements:
- Money: he needs at least the action's price.
- Shopping for an item needs more than {KEEP_CENTS}.
- Selling needs something in his bag.
- Attributes: some need a minimum strength, charm, wits or luck, counting his gear. For example arm-wrestling needs strength 4 and volunteering as a museum guide needs wits 6.
- Level: some need a minimum level.
- Time of day, by the real sun over his city: morning {TIME_MORNING}, day {TIME_DAY}, evening {TIME_EVENING}, night {TIME_NIGHT}, and just after midnight {TIME_MIDNIGHT}, where {SUNRISE_AT} is sunrise and {SUNSET_AT} is sunset (see Day and night). Bars serve rakia only in the evening, for example.
- Season: some only happen in one season, such as mulled wine in winter (see Seasons).
- Weather: some need wet weather (rain, snow or a storm), snow, heat, frost or sunshine, such as building a snowman (see Weather).
- Low meters: some only happen when a meter is low, such as napping under a tree when his energy is under 40, or the soup kitchen when his fullness is under 30.
## Money {coins}
His money is in euros, counted to the cent and shown like {EXAMPLE_PRICE}. He starts with {START_CENTS}. Money never goes below €0.

Prices are real-world euro prices, the same in every country: a coffee is a few euros, a hostel bed about €28, a bike from {CHEAP_BIKE} and a car from {CHEAP_CAR}. Wages start from real ones too, but grow as he levels up (see Wages).

He spends money on food, drinks, tickets, trips and gear, and earns it from jobs, from selling items and from lucky finds. While he has less than {SKINT} money is a need, steeper the closer he gets to €0, and he leans away from spending (see His needs).

He keeps a small reserve: he won't buy gear that would leave him with less than {KEEP_CENTS}. Until he has a dragon he saves up for his next vehicle (see Saving for a vehicle). Waking up in hospital costs {HOSPITAL_FEE}, but never more than {HOSPITAL_MAX_PCT} of his money. Each week he pays his bills (see Bills).
## Bills {bills}
At the start of each week (Monday at midnight by your phone's clock), his bills arrive, also while you're away, and he pays them by himself:
{BILL_LIST}

At level 1 they come to {BILLS_L1} a week, at level 10 {BILLS_L10} and at level 30 {BILLS_L30}. If he hasn't got it all, he pays nothing and a debt collector takes the cheapest thing in his bag instead, never his gear or a treasured item (see The bag and selling). With nothing to take, the bills are written off. The Stats sheet shows this week's bills on the Level card.
## Wages {wages}
Every job lists a real-world wage for a shift, such as a café paying for washing cups. At level 1 a job with no attribute need pays exactly that, unless the daily forecast raises pay for the day (see Daily forecast). As he grows he earns more, well above real wages, so that vehicles come within reach:
- {WAGE_LEVEL_PCT} more for every level above 1,
- {WAGE_ATTR_PCT} more for every point of the attribute the job needs, counting his gear. Tending the bar needs charm, so with charm 10 it pays {WAGE_ATTR_X10} more.
- up to {VISA_PAY_MAX} more with a better visa in the country (see Visas).
- {EMO_BONUS_PCT} more when the job needs his emotion's attribute (see Emotions), and {SILVER_PCT} more with Silver tongue (see Lifetime rewards).

For example a job with no attribute need pays {WAGE_LV11} its listed wage at level 11, and {WAGE_LV30} at level 30. Only pay grows: prices stay the same at every level. Anything that pays counts, including bets, gigs and lucky finds at places such as the coin by the ATM. Selling items and money found while walking don't grow.
## Chance cards at work {work_cards}
After {WORK_CARD_PCT} of his paid shifts (jobs of {SHIFT_MIN} minutes or more, not bets or lucky finds), a chance card comes up: a small dilemma at work. He picks by himself and you never have to. A higher attribute makes him lean to the bold choice ({INC_LEAN_PCT} for each point above 1, up to {INC_LEAN_CAP_PCT} past even). The bold choice works out {WORK_WIN_BASE_PCT} of the time, plus {WORK_WIN_PER_PCT} for each point of the same attribute, up to {WORK_WIN_MAX_PCT}; otherwise it goes wrong. Pay is a share of the wage he just earned, and he is never docked more than he has. The feed shows what he chose and how it went:
{WORK_CARD_LIST}
## Saving for a vehicle {saving}
He always saves up for the cheapest vehicle one step above what he rides:
- on foot, a {GOAL_BIKE} for {CHEAP_BIKE},
- on a bike, a {GOAL_SCOOTER} for {CHEAP_SCOOTER},
- on a scooter or motorbike, a {GOAL_CAR} for {CHEAP_CAR},
- in a car, a {GOAL_DRAGON} for {CHEAP_DRAGON} (see Fantasy mounts).

A fantasy mount counts as the vehicle it sits beside: on a tortoise he saves for a scooter, on a broomstick for a car, on a carpet for a dragon. With a dragon there is nothing more to save for. He saves up to the price plus his reserve of {KEEP_CENTS}, and for the dragon its first day of gold ({DRAGON_UPKEEP}) too. With a dragon, on a day it hasn't eaten yet he saves up to its gold plus his reserve, so it doesn't sulk for long. Until he has it, money is a need of at least {SAVING_URGE} urge and spending weighs {SAVING_SPEND_PCT} as much (see His needs), so paid work and places that pay pull him.

Once he has the money (and isn't still saving for a trip fare), a shop that sells that vehicle weighs {GOAL_SHOP_PULL} times as much as an ordinary place when he picks his next goal, so he heads for one, and browsing there gets +{GOAL_SHOP}. A bike shop or vehicle dealer shows him {DEALER_OFFERS} random vehicles and he buys the best one he can afford while keeping his reserve. If all of them cost too much (a better material or modifiers raise the price), he walks on and tries again later. The Stats sheet shows his goal under Saving for, and with a dragon, its gold on a day it hasn't eaten yet.
## Buffs and debuffs {buffs}
Some actions leave an effect that lasts a while, shown with the minutes left on the Stats sheet. Buffs count down in game minutes, while the game is open and through the time away it catches up (see While you were away).

A buff can change a meter every hour it lasts (Caffeinated: +5 energy per hour; Healthy: +1 health per hour; Hungover: -3 energy and -2 mood per hour), or multiply the XP he gets from actions (Inspired: double XP). Several XP buffs multiply together. A buff can also raise an attribute while it lasts: Lucky (from a big tip or rubbing a statue) adds {LUCKY_LUCK} luck.

Doing an action that gives a buff he already has starts that buff over at its full length instead of stacking it.

# ❤ Body stats
## Health {health}
Health (HP, the red bar) goes from 0 to 100 and starts at {START_HEALTH}.

He loses health when he is starving or parched: {STARVING_PER_H} health per hour while fullness is at 0, and another {STARVING_PER_H} per hour while thirst is at 0. Some risky actions and drinks also cost a little health.

He gets health back from hospitals, doctors, pharmacies, rest and good food, and from buffs such as Healthy.

When health reaches 0 he faints and wakes up in hospital (see Fainting and hospital).
## Energy {energy}
Energy (EN, the yellow bar) goes from 0 to 100 and starts at {START_ENERGY}.

He uses {ENERGY_PER_KM} energy for every km he walks ({WEARY_PCT} less with Never weary: see Lifetime rewards), less on a bike or in a motor vehicle (see Speed and vehicles), less on a tailwind day (see Daily forecast), and {COLD_ENERGY_PER_H} more every hour while it is freezing (see Weather). Coffee, food, rest, naps and buffs such as Caffeinated give it back; hard jobs, workouts and debuffs such as Hungover or Crash take it away.

When energy is under {NEED_AT} he needs rest more and more (see His needs), and shuns actions that tire him. Napping under a tree only happens when it is under {NAP_BELOW}. Under {TIRED_ENERGY} he slows down, to half speed at 0, but he never stops. Nothing else happens at 0 energy.
## Fullness {fullness}
Fullness (FU, the green bar) is how fed he is, from 0 to 100, starting at {START_FULLNESS}.

It drops by {FULLNESS_PER_H} every hour the game is open, so from full it lasts about {FULLNESS_HOURS} hours. Meals, snacks and buffs such as Snacking fill it.

Under {NEED_AT} he needs food more and more (see His needs); under {URGENT_AT} he heads straight for it. At 0 he starts losing {STARVING_PER_H} health per hour until he eats.
## Thirst {thirst}
Thirst is how well watered he is, from 0 to 100 (higher is better), starting at {START_THIRST}.

It drops by {THIRST_PER_H} every hour the game is open, so from full it lasts about {THIRST_HOURS} hours, and {HOT_THIRST} times as fast in the heat (see Weather). Drinks, drinking fountains, cafes and bars fill it.

Under {NEED_AT} he needs a drink more and more (see His needs); under {URGENT_AT} he heads straight for one. At 0 he starts losing {STARVING_PER_H} health per hour until he drinks.
## Mood {mood}
Mood is how happy he is, from 0 to 100, starting at {START_MOOD}. The daily forecast can make one kind of place lift it more for a day (see Daily forecast).

It drops by {MOOD_PER_H} every hour the game is open, and by {DIRTY_MOOD_PER_H} more while he is dirty (see Hygiene). Wet weather takes {WET_MOOD_PER_H} more an hour and sunshine gives {SUNNY_MOOD_PER_H} back (see Weather). Moodlets add their bit every hour too (see Moodlets). Treats, sights, music, chats and buffs such as Tipsy or Blessed raise it; failed bets, risks gone wrong and debuffs such as Hungover lower it.

When mood is under {NEED_AT} he needs cheering up more and more (see His needs).

Mood scales the XP he gets from actions: ×{MOOD_XP_MIN} at mood 0, ×1 at 50 and ×{MOOD_XP_MAX} at 100, in a straight line between. For example at mood 80 an action worth 20 XP gives {MOOD_80_XP}. The Stats sheet shows the multiplier now. First-visit XP is not scaled.
## Moodlets {moodlets}
On top of the usual drift, named moodlets nudge his mood a little every hour while they last: "Wish come true", "Rattled", "Scared of the dog park". Each has its own mood an hour and a time left. The same moodlet again starts its time over instead of adding up, and he keeps at most {MAX_MOODLETS}.

Most moodlets also count towards an emotion (see Emotions). The Mind card on the Stats sheet opens a Mood list of everything that moves his mood each hour now: the drift, dirt, the weather and each moodlet, with their total.
## Wants and fears {wants}
He always has two wants, each for a kind of place: a long one, a sight he dreams of ({LONG_WANTS}), and a short one, an everyday treat he wants within {SHORT_H} hours or he moves on to another. After a mishap he also wants to cheer up somewhere for {SHAKE_H} hours. The Mind card on the Stats sheet lists them.

A wanted kind of place pulls his goal {WANT_PULL} times as hard (see Picking the next goal). Doing anything there makes the wish come true: {WANT_XP_LONG} XP and {WISH_MOOD_LONG} for a long want, {WANT_XP_SHORT} XP and {WISH_MOOD_SHORT} for a short one, {WANT_XP_SHAKE} XP and {WISH_MOOD_SHAKE} for cheering up. Then a new want takes its place.

A risk gone wrong rattles him ({RATTLED}), and {FEAR_PCT} of the time leaves him wary of that kind of place ({SHAKEN}), up to {MAX_FEARS} fears. A kind he already fears, or one where a wish came true on the same visit, leaves no new fear. A feared kind pulls only ×{FEAR_PULL}, but he also wants to face it again, so the two even out. A visit there with no mishap beats the fear: {WANT_XP_FACE} XP and {FACED_MOOD}.

Each wish that comes true also gives lifetime points (see Lifetime rewards).
## Emotions {emotions}
His moodlets add up to one emotion, shown on the Character card of the Stats sheet and, unless he is just Fine, on the top card next to the city. Each moodlet counts its mood an hour towards its own emotion; ones with no emotion of their own count towards Happy when they lift his mood and Sad when they lower it. Energy of {FULL_ENERGY} or more counts {ENERGY_WEIGHT} towards Energised, and mood of {SAD_MOOD} or less {SAD_WEIGHT} towards Sad. The biggest wins. With nothing to count he is Bored when mood is under {BORED_MOOD}, else just Fine.

Doing anything at some kinds of place brings an emotion's moodlet ({EMO_MOODLET}):
- Focused ("Deep in thought"): {FOCUSED_KINDS}. Lifts Wits work.
- Inspired: {INSPIRED_KINDS}. Lifts Luck work.
- Playful ("In a playful mood"): {PLAYFUL_KINDS}. Lifts Charm work.
- Energised ("Pumped"): {ENERGISED_KINDS}. Lifts Strength work.
Rattled and Scared moodlets count towards Tense, and so does a taste he can't stand.

An emotion pulls his goal ×{EMO_PULL} towards its kinds of place, and when sad, tense or bored to places that help: Sad to {SAD_KINDS}; Tense to {TENSE_KINDS}; Bored to {BORED_KINDS}. Focused, Inspired, Playful and Energised also weigh actions that train or need their attribute ×{EMO_PULL}, and give those actions {EMO_BONUS_PCT} more XP; jobs that need the attribute pay {EMO_BONUS_PCT} more. Happy gives {HAPPY_XP_PCT} more XP from every action.
## Hidden tastes {tastes}
Every walker is born with {LIKES} kinds of place he loves and {DISLIKES} he can't stand, picked at random from {TASTE_KINDS}. Nobody knows them at first, not even him: the first time he does something at one, the feed says so ("Turns out he loves zoos"), and the Character card on the Stats sheet lists it from then on.

Known or not, a kind he loves pulls his goal ×{LIKE_PULL} and one he can't stand ×{DISLIKE_PULL} (see Picking the next goal). Each visit to a kind he loves gives {LIKE_MOOD}; one he can't stand gives {DISLIKE_MOOD}, which counts towards Tense (see Emotions).
## Lifetime rewards {perks}
Wishes that come true give lifetime points: {POINTS_LONG} for a long want, {POINTS_SHORT} for a short one, {POINTS_SHAKE} for cheering up, {POINTS_FACE} for facing a fear (see Wants and fears). Each level gives {LEVEL_POINTS}.

He spends them himself on lasting perks, as soon as he has enough for one. When he can afford more than one he takes the one tied to his best attribute (perks with no attribute count his average), the cheaper on a tie:
{PERK_LIST}
The Character card on the Stats sheet shows his points, the price of the cheapest perk left, and the perks he has.
## Hygiene {hygiene}
Hygiene is how clean he is, from 0 to 100, starting at {START_HYGIENE}.

It drops by {HYGIENE_PER_H} every hour the game is open. Showers, swimming, hotel rooms, the laundry, a spa and the toilets raise it; sweaty jobs and workouts lower it.

When hygiene is under {NEED_AT} he needs a wash more and more (see His needs).

Under {DIRTY_AT} he is dirty, and two things happen. His charm counts less, down to {DIRTY_CHARM_PCT} less at 0 hygiene, which means worse sell prices and fewer charm actions. And his mood drops {DIRTY_MOOD_PER_H} more every hour. The Stats sheet shows charm's drop in red.
# 🌦 Weather and sky
## Weather {weather}
The weather in his city is the real weather there, from Open-Meteo. The game asks for it once every {WEATHER_TTL_MIN} minutes while it is open, and gets the hours from today to the end of tomorrow in one small request. While you are away the weather doesn't count (see While you were away). The last forecast is kept on the device. Until the first forecast arrives the weather is unknown and changes nothing. If Open-Meteo can't be reached later, he goes on by the last forecast until it runs out at the end of its tomorrow.

What the weather does to him:
- Wet (rain, snow or a storm): outdoor places such as parks, gardens and sports pitches pull his goal {WET_OUTDOOR} times as much, and shelters such as museums, cafes, libraries and cinemas {WET_SHELTER} times. His mood drops {WET_MOOD_PER_H} more an hour.
- Snow: on foot or by bike he goes at {SNOW_SPEED_PCT} of his speed.
- Hot ({HOT_C}°C or more): he gets thirsty {HOT_THIRST} times as fast, and ice cream, fountains and pools pull his goal {HOT_COOL} times as much.
- Freezing ({COLD_C}°C or less): he loses {COLD_ENERGY_PER_H} energy more an hour.
- Sunny (clear, {SUNNY_MIN_C}°C to below {HOT_C}°C, while the sky isn't yet dark): his mood gets {SUNNY_MOOD_PER_H} back an hour.

On a trip to another city by bus, plane or car the weather doesn't reach him (driving his car around town, it does).

Some actions only happen in the right weather, such as hot chocolate while it pours, a snowman in the park or watching the sunset (see Requirements). The top card and the Stats sheet show the weather now.
## Seasons {seasons}
The season comes from the date in his city: spring is March to May, summer June to August, autumn September to November and winter December to February. South of the equator they are the other way round, and within {TROPICS_LAT}° of the equator it is summer all year.

Some actions only happen in one season, such as mulled wine by a bar's heater in winter, kicking through leaves in autumn or a picnic under the blossom in spring. There are {SKY_ACTIONS} actions that need a season or a weather. The Stats sheet shows the season.
## Day and night {day_night}
Day and night follow the real sun over his city, worked out on your device from the date and where he is, with no download. Times go by the city's own clock, not your phone's: when it is night in Tokyo it is night for him in Tokyo. The clock comes with the weather, and until then from the city's longitude.

For actions, sunrise counts as {SUNRISE_AT} and sunset as {SUNSET_AT}, with the day stretched or squeezed to the {HALF_DAY_H} hours between them and the night to the rest, so "evening" starts around sunset in every season. In the far north and south, where the sun may not rise or set, a day counts as at least {MIN_DAY_H} and at most {MAX_DAY_H} hours.

After sunset the map darkens under a blue shade as the sun sinks, until it is {NIGHT_SHADE_PCT} dark once the sun is {NIGHT_DEG}° below the horizon, and lightens again at dawn. At night he may stroll under the street lamps.
## Opening hours {opening}
Shops, offices, museums, galleries, libraries, banks, cafes and other day businesses are open from {OPEN_H} to {CLOSE_H} by the city's clock. Kiosks and corner shops, bars, pubs, fast food, hotels, hospitals, pharmacies, stations, parks and monuments stay open all night.

On a public holiday (see Public holidays) the day businesses stay shut all day.

He never sets off for a closed place (it weighs 0 when he picks his next goal, and the story waits for it too: a quest step there holds the quest until it opens, a quest giver or event there isn't picked while it's shut (an event can run out meanwhile), and no new event starts at a closed place). If a place closes while he is on his way, he finds it closed, does nothing there (no action and no first-visit XP) and moves on after the usual minute.

## Magic {magic}
Magic (the indigo bar) goes from 0 to {MAGIC_MAX} and starts at 0. It never drops by itself. Walking along ley lines and reaching the places they join fill it (see Ley lines), enchanted gear as he walks (see The shop after midnight), a strange story from a nap in a fairy ring (see Fairy rings), and a few fantasy quests and events, such as The Ley Hunter, humming stones and a circle of mushrooms; his spells and the shop after midnight spend it (see Spells). Nothing happens when it is empty, except that he can't cast.
# 💪 Attributes
## Strength {str}
Strength starts at 1. It grows a little from physical actions (workouts, hauling, sport), from levelling up, and from gear such as weapons, shields, helmets, boots and backpacks.

Strength unlocks physical actions: for example hauling for the butcher needs 3, arm-wrestling bets 4, loading cargo at the harbour 5, a lifeguard shift 6. Those mostly pay well.

The number counted for requirements is his own strength plus every strength bonus on his gear.
## Charm {cha}
Charm starts at 1. It grows from social actions (chatting, performing, parties), from levelling up, and from gear such as clothes, bracelets, earrings and instruments. A finished city card set adds {SET_CHARM} while he is in that country (see City cards).

Charm unlocks more actions than any other attribute: chatting with the barista needs 3, auditioning at the theatre needs 6, and many social and selling actions need some.

Charm also gets him better prices when he sells: each point adds {SELL_PER_CHA_PCT} to what a shop pays (see The bag and selling).
## Wits {wit}
Wits start at 1. They grow from reading, learning, games, puzzles and city trivia, from levelling up, and from gear such as bows, beanies, wrenches, pocket watches and violins.

Wits unlock clever actions: fixing bikes needs 2, sketching an exhibit or fixing a printer 3, a freelance gig 4, volunteering as a museum guide 6.
## Luck {luck}
Luck starts at 1. It grows a tiny bit from lucky finds, from levelling up, and from gear such as rings, amulets, lucky charms, hoodies and sandals.

Luck does four things:
- Fewer mishaps: each point makes an action's risk {LUCK_RISK_PCT} less likely, up to {LUCK_RISK_CAP_PCT} less. At luck 5 a 20% risk becomes {LUCK5_RISK}. With Iron stomach, food and drink never go wrong (see Lifetime rewards).
- Fewer bad incidents: the same cut makes an incident less likely to be a bad one (see The storyteller).
- Rarer items: each point lifts the shop's rarity roll (see Modifiers and rarity).
- Money on the street: every minute he walks there is a {FIND_PER_LUCK_PCT} chance per point of luck (at most {FIND_CAP_PCT}, more on a leaky-pockets day: see Daily forecast, and {LUCKY_FIND} times as much with Lucky streak: see Lifetime rewards) that he finds {FIND_MIN} to {FIND_MAX}. It shows in his History as "Found €0.50 on the street".
It also unlocks actions that need it, such as finding a coin by the ATM (luck 2). Gear and the Lucky buff count.
# ⭐ Progress
## XP and levels {xp}
He gains XP (experience) from almost every action, and {FIRST_VISIT_XP} XP the first time he reaches each named place. The first-visit bonus isn't changed by buffs, only by the daily forecast (see Daily forecast); XP from actions is multiplied by buffs such as Inspired, by his emotion (see Emotions) and by Quick study (see Lifetime rewards).

Each level needs 100 x his level in XP: {XP_LV1} XP from level 1 to 2, {XP_LV2} XP from 2 to 3, {XP_LV10} XP from 10 to 11. Extra XP carries over to the next level.

Every time he levels up, his highest attribute (not counting gear) grows by 1, and he gets {SPEED_PER_LEVEL_PCT} faster. He also gets a free pack of city cards (see City cards). On a tie, strength comes first, then charm, wits and luck.

A higher level unlocks better materials for the gear he finds (see Materials) and some actions.
## Records {records}
The Stats sheet ends with his records over his whole life. Each best only goes up:
- Longest walk without a stop: distance between two places he stopped at (a faint starts it over too)
- Most walked, and most places stopped at, in one day
- Most money he has held at once
- Longest he has gone without fainting

It also counts the countries and cities he has been to, his mishaps (an action that went wrong) and his faints. The time away counts the same as time watched.

When he beats a best he may say so in his History ("New record: … in one day!"), at most once a day, and only once that best is worth it: at least {REC_STRETCH_KM} km without a stop, {REC_DAY_KM} km in a day, {REC_STOPS} places in a day, {REC_CENTS} held, or {REC_NOFAINT} without fainting.
## Fainting and hospital {fainting}
When his health reaches 0 he faints. He wakes up at the nearest hospital the game knows of in the city, or where he is if no hospital has loaded.

Waking up costs {HOSPITAL_FEE}, but never more than {HOSPITAL_MAX_PCT} of his money. His health is set to {WAKE_HEALTH}, and his energy, fullness and thirst are raised to at least 30 if they were lower. He then stays in the hospital for {STAY_MIN} minute before walking on. You'll see "Fainted! Woke up at …" in his History.
# 🎒 Items and gear
## Gear and items {items}
He finds items by browsing shops: weapon and hunting shops sell weapons and armour, hardware stores sell tools he can swing, clothes and shoe shops sell clothes, jewellers and gift shops sell trinkets, music shops sell instruments, and bike shops and vehicle dealers sell vehicles (see Speed and vehicles). Now and then a pet, hardware or carpet shop has a fantasy mount, and a castle's stable sells dragons (see Fantasy mounts).

Each item fits one slot: weapon, offhand, head, body, feet, bag, trinket or vehicle. He wears at most one item per slot. There is no fighting: gear only adds to his attributes, and vehicles to his speed.

Every browse shows him one random item (a bike shop or vehicle dealer shows several: see Saving for a vehicle). He buys it only if it beats what he wears in that slot, meaning its bonuses add up to more, and only if it leaves him at least {KEEP_CENTS}. If he buys it, he puts it on at once and the old item goes into his bag.

An item's name tells you what it is made of: modifiers before, then the material, then the base item, then a suffix, for example "Mighty Bronze Sword of the Fox".
## Materials {materials}
Every item is made of a material, picked at random from those his level has unlocked. Metal items and cloth items use different names for the same steps:
- level 1: Wooden / Cotton (power 1) and Copper / Wool (power 2)
- level 3: Bronze / Linen (power 3)
- level 5: Iron / Leather (power 4)
- level 10: Steel / Silk (power 6)
- level 15: Silver / Velvet (power 7)
- level 25: Mithril / Dragonhide (power 10)

The item's main attribute grows by {POWER_TO_STAT} for each point of power: a Steel Sword gives strength +3. Prices are real: a plain Bronze Sword costs {BRONZE_SWORD}. Material changes the price: Wooden or Cotton is half that, Steel or Silk twice, Mithril or Dragonhide ten times.
## Modifiers and rarity {modifiers}
Items can have 0 to 3 modifiers, and the number decides the rarity:
- Common, no modifiers: {MODS0_PCT} of items
- Uncommon, 1 modifier: {MODS1_PCT}
- Rare, 2 modifiers: {MODS2_PCT}
- Legendary, 3 modifiers: {MODS3_PCT}

Those are the odds at luck 0. Each point of luck lifts the roll by {LUCK_RARITY_PCT}, up to {LUCK_RARITY_CAP_PCT}, so a lucky walker finds rarer items more often.

The first modifier is always a suffix: of the Bear (strength +2), of the Fox (wits +2), of the Peacock (charm +2), of Fortune (luck +2) or of the Owl (wits +1, luck +1). The others are prefixes, never the same one twice: Mighty (strength +2), Charming (charm +2), Clever (wits +2), Lucky (luck +2), Sharp (strength +1, wits +1) or Gleaming (charm +1, luck +1).

Each modifier adds {MOD_PRICE_PCT} of the price. Enchantments are modifiers too, sold only by the shop after midnight (see The shop after midnight).
## The bag and selling {bag}
Gear he takes off goes into his bag, which holds {BAG_SIZE} items. The Stats sheet lists what is in it.

At shops he can "Sell his loot": he sells the cheapest item in his bag. A shop pays {SELL_PCT} of the item's price, plus {SELL_PER_CHA_PCT} of that for every point of charm. For example an item worth €100 sells for {SELL_100_CHA0} with charm 0, or {SELL_100_CHA5} with charm 5. The daily forecast can raise it for a day (see Daily forecast), and a rascal gets more (see Kind or rascal).

While his bag is full he goes on a selling run: resale shops ({RESALE_SHOPS}) pull his next goal like an empty need (see Picking the next goal). There "Sell his loot" sells his cheapest things one after another until {SELL_RUN_LEFT} are left, with a line for each sale. Other shops still buy just one thing at a time.

If his bag is full when another item comes in before he finds such a shop, he sells the cheapest item he may sell on the spot, which can be the new one.

He never sells his {TREASURED_KEEP} most travelled treasured items (see Item stories), unless they are all that is left in the bag.
# ✈ Travel

## Portals {portals}
Very rarely, an old stone he reaches opens like a door. Each time he reaches a real monument or castle ruin, there is a {PORTAL_PCT} chance it opens, but never within {PORTAL_GAP_DAYS} days of his last portal, and never while he is on a quest. He steps through at once and for free, and comes out in a real city he has never been to, anywhere in the world (picked from the listed cities, weighted by population). The feed says where he went in and came out, and his life story keeps his first portal. It works the same while you're away, but not at the made-up places of a city with no places loaded. The Portal hopper badge (Collection, Feats tab) counts the portals he has gone through.
## Trips to other cities {trips}
Every time he picks a new goal he rolls the trip dice, unless a need is urgent (see His needs) or he is on a quest (see Quests):
- {COUNTRY_PCT}: he travels to a city in another country,
- {CITY_PCT}: he travels to another city in the same country,
- otherwise he stays and picks a place in this city.

Apart from the dice, an old monument or castle ruin very rarely opens as a portal and takes him to a new city for free (see Portals).

While no places of note are loaded in the city box, the odds double to {NO_PLACES_COUNTRY_PCT} and {NO_PLACES_CITY_PCT}, so he doesn't stay long in a city where he can only walk to random spots. On the last day of his visa a trip abroad gets likelier still (see Visas).

The first goal after you choose a city never rolls the dice. The country he is in is the country of the nearest of the {CITY_COUNT} listed cities ({COUNTRY_COUNT} countries). Cities closer than {SAME_CITY_KM} km don't count as a different city. The city is picked at random, weighted by population, so big cities come up most.

While he walks to the station, where he is going stays a surprise: the History says only "somewhere in Bulgaria" or "somewhere abroad", and he reveals the city when he boards. You may guess it (see Guess the next trip). While you are away he doesn't keep it secret.

He first walks to a bus or railway station, a ferry terminal, an airport, or (with a car) a gas station, and pays there (see Stations and getting there, and Fares). Cycling and hitchhiking set off from where he stands and are free (see Other ways to travel), and so does a long walk, paying only for hostels (see Long walks). If there is no way out or he can't pay, he stays, the Debug log says why, and he saves up for it (see Fares), unless he is broke enough to hitchhike.

In the new city he picks goals within 8 x the square root of its population in metres from the centre, at least {MIN_TRIP_CITY_KM} km and at most {MAX_TRIP_CITY_KM} km.
## Visas {visas}
Each country he spends days in gives him a better visa there. A day counts at the first minute of each day, by your phone's clock, in the country he is in, also while you're away:
{VISA_LIST}

A visa limits how long one stay in the country lasts; a stay starts when he enters it. Nothing makes him leave: on the last day of the stay, a trip abroad is {VISA_URGE} times likelier when he rolls the trip dice (see Trips to other cities), and past it {VISA_PUSH} times, so he moves on by himself. ## Stations and getting there {stations}
How he goes depends on the distance in a straight line:
- under {PLANE_FROM_KM} km he always goes by road,
- from {PLANE_FROM_KM} to {PLANE_ALWAYS_KM} km the chance of flying grows steadily,
- over {PLANE_ALWAYS_KM} km he always flies.

By road he takes the bus from the nearest bus station among the places loaded around him, or the train or a ferry instead (see Other ways to travel). If he owns a car, he walks to the nearest gas station instead (EV chargers don't count), fills up and drives. Roads longer than {MAX_ROAD_KM} km don't count: buses and cars don't cross oceans.

To fly he walks to the nearest airport with scheduled flights within {AIRPORT_KM} km ({AIRPORT_COUNT} airports worldwide, plus the made-up ones of big cities, see Made-up places), and lands at the airport nearest the new city. If there is no airport at either end, or he can't pay the flight, he goes by road, and the other way round.

The bus and the car follow the real road route (from the OSRM route planner) at {BUS_KMH} km/h and {CAR_ROAD_KMH} km/h, and so do the train, the bike and a hitched lift at their own speeds (see Other ways to travel); a ferry sails straight. A plane flies straight at {PLANE_KMH} km/h. These are real speeds, but rides go {RIDE_SPEEDUP} times faster and never take longer than {RIDE_MAX_MIN} minutes of the game being open (a long walk is slower: see Long walks). So 100 km by bus takes about {BUS_100_MIN} minutes, and 300 km by bus about {BUS_300_MIN}. While he rides, the map zooms out and a red line marks the way. Every ride but the plane arrives in the new city's centre; a plane at its airport. Then the new city's streets load and he walks on.
## Fares {fares}
Fares are in euros, and only cycling, hitchhiking and a long walk under {HOSTEL_KM} km of road are free (a rascal may also skip a bus ticket: see Kind or rascal):
- bus: {BUS_PER_KM} per km of road,
- plane: {PLANE_BASE} plus {PLANE_PER_KM} per km flown,
- car: fuel at {FUEL_PER_KM} per km of road,
- train: {TRAIN_PER_KM} per km of road,
- ferry: {FERRY_PER_KM} per km over the water,
- cycling and hitchhiking: free (see Other ways to travel).
- long walk: {HOSTEL_PRICE} a hostel night for every {HOSTEL_KM} km of road, so free under {HOSTEL_KM} km (see Long walks).

Road distance is the straight line x {ROAD_FACTOR}. So 300 km by bus costs about {BUS_300}, and a 1,500 km flight about {PLANE_1500}, before his souvenirs or the daily forecast make it cheaper (see Souvenirs and Daily forecast).

He pays at the station, or as he sets off on a long walk. If the dice send him on a trip he can't pay for, he hitchhikes when he has less than {HITCH_BELOW}; otherwise he stays this time and saves up: until he has the fare, money is a need of at least {SAVING_URGE} urge (see His needs), and spending counts {SAVING_SPEND_PCT} as much. The next trip roll he can pay for takes him. He also saves the same way for his next vehicle (see Saving for a vehicle), so paid work can stay favoured after he has the fare. If he spends too much on the way to the station, he turns back and saves up the same way.
## Other ways to travel {modes}
Besides the bus, the plane and his own car, he picks other ways on his own when the places around him allow it (and now and then walks: see Long walks):
- {BIKE_ICON} Bike: on a bike, he cycles to a town under {CYCLE_MAX_KM} km away instead of taking a station, for free, at {BIKE_ROAD_KMH} km/h. He arrives tired: {BIKE_EFFORT_KM} energy per km ({WEARY_PCT} less with Never weary: see Lifetime rewards).
- {FERRY_ICON} Ferry: with a ferry terminal loaded, a trip by road to a city up to {FERRY_MAX_KM} km away in a straight line goes by ferry {FERRY_PCT} of the time. It sails straight over the water at {FERRY_KMH} km/h and lands in the new city's centre.
- {TRAIN_ICON} Train: with a railway station loaded, a road trip of at least {TRAIN_FROM_KM} km goes by train instead of the bus, at {TRAIN_KMH} km/h. From {SLEEPER_FROM_KM} km it is a night train: he sleeps in a berth and arrives with full energy.
- {HITCH_ICON} Hitchhiking: with less than {HITCH_BELOW} and no fare he can pay (or no station to leave from), he doesn't wait to save up, as long as a road reaches (up to {MAX_ROAD_KM} km). He sticks his thumb out right where he stands and gets a free lift at {HITCH_KMH} km/h, counting the wait. {HITCH_BAD_PCT} of lifts go badly ({HITCH_BAD_FX}), {HITCH_GOOD_PCT} are a great story ({HITCH_GOOD_FX}), and the rest are just a quiet ride.

A car owner drives rather than taking the train or a ferry, unless he can't pay the fuel and hitchhikes (see Stations and getting there). Rides of every kind go {RIDE_SPEEDUP} times faster than real and are capped the same way, apart from a long walk (see Long walks).
## Long walks {walks}
Now and then, when he has no vehicle, a trip to a town {LONG_WALK_MIN_KM} to {LONG_WALK_MAX_KM} km away (in a straight line) becomes a long walk instead: {LONG_WALK_PCT} of the time, if he can pay the hostels. {WALK_ICON} He sets off on foot from where he stands, along the road the bus would take.

A walk is far slower than any ride: it lasts {LONG_WALK_MAX_H} hours, and time away counts towards it (if you leave mid-walk he keeps walking, and is still on his way if you come back before it ends). The map stays zoomed out with the red line, as on a ride, and no streets download on the way, so it costs the phone no more than a ride.

He sleeps in a hostel every {HOSTEL_KM} km of road, at {HOSTEL_PRICE} a night, paid as he sets off. The hostels' meals and showers keep his food, drink and hygiene where they were while he walks (the On the trail buff). At the end he gets {LONG_WALK_XP_PER_KM} XP per km and +{LONG_WALK_MOOD} mood. While you are away he walks too, if the walk fits in the time you were gone; otherwise he doesn't set off this time. The hostel beds keep his energy up too, and a long walk, like any trip, doesn't count towards his walking records or the km in his week.

As he boards for another city he packs a travel kit on his own, by need, and pays for it on top of the fare:
{KIT_LIST}

## Jet lag {jetlag}
After a flight he counts the time zones he crossed: one for every {DEG_PER_ZONE} degrees of longitude between the two airports, the short way round the globe. For each one he is Jet-lagged for {JET_LAG_H_PER_ZONE} hour, at most {JET_LAG_MAX_H} hours. While it lasts he loses {JET_LAG_ENERGY_PER_H} energy an hour and gets {JET_LAG_XP_PCT} less XP from actions (see Buffs and debuffs). A flight within one time zone, and every ride on the ground, gives no jet lag (though cycling, a night train, a hitched lift or a long walk change his meters in their own ways: see Other ways to travel and Long walks).
## Crossing a border {borders}
Each time he arrives in a different country than the one he left, by any kind of trip, the feed and History get a few border lines:
- a passport stamp, with how many countries he has been to (the Collection's countries list shows them all),
- some euros swapped for the local money, just to hold: prices in the game stay in euros,
- on his first visit to that country, the first word he learns there, such as "Zdravey" (hello) in Bulgaria,
- a dry remark about the border guard.

{BORDER_COUNTRIES} countries have their own word and money. Elsewhere he still gets the stamp and the guard. It all happens on its own, while you are away too, and changes nothing about his stats.
## Local words {words}
In {WORD_COUNTRIES} countries he picks up everyday words in the local language ({WORD_LANGUAGES} languages, about {WORD_EACH} words each): hello, thank you, coffee, bread, the bill, cheers and the like. At {WORD_PCT} of his stops there he learns one he doesn't know yet, with how to say it in Latin letters where the language uses another script, for example "🗣 Learned a Bulgarian word at Cafe Rila: "кафе (kafe)" means coffee".

A word that fits the place comes first: coffee at a café, bread at a bakery, the bill at a restaurant, beer and cheers at a bar or pub, water at a fountain, a church (or mosque or temple) at a place of worship, station at a station, pharmacy at a pharmacy, ice cream at an ice cream parlour. Words like hello and thank you he learns anywhere. Each word gives wits +{WORD_WIT} and {WORD_XP} XP. Words carry over between countries that share a language, and he learns them while you are away too. The Stats sheet lists them, and the Polyglot badge counts them (see Badges and titles).
## Country flavour {flavour}
{FLAVOUR_COUNTRIES} countries have their own actions ({FLAVOUR_ACTIONS} in all), such as banitsa with boza at a bakery in Bulgaria or Turkish coffee in a copper pot. They join the usual actions at the matching kind of place while he is in that country.

To make them come up often, each one gets +{FLAVOUR_WEIGHT} weight when he chooses (see How he chooses). They follow the same requirements as every other action.
## City trivia {trivia}
When he reaches a city, the game looks it up on Wikidata: the most populous place within {TRIVIA_KM} km of the centre, and facts about it such as when it was founded, how many people live there, how high it is, up to {TRIVIA_TWINS} twin towns, who it is named after and what it is the capital of.

Each fact becomes a quick action he can do at any place in that city, for example "Learn that Sofia is named after Saint Sofia". Each gives wits +{TRIVIA_WIT} and {TRIVIA_XP} XP. If Wikidata can't be reached, there is simply no trivia for that city.
## Public holidays {holidays}
The game knows the public holidays of the country he is in, from Nager.Date (a free holiday calendar). It asks once per country per year and keeps the list on your phone, so it costs one small download a year. Only holidays for the whole country count, not regional ones, and the day goes by the city's clock.

On a holiday the feed says so once, with the holiday's local name, for example "It's Liberation Day (Ден на Освобождението) here, a public holiday". All day the day businesses stay shut (see Opening hours), and holiday actions join what he can do at every place: watching a parade, a family picnic, a toast with strangers, the bells and speeches, and fireworks at night. They follow the same requirements and weights as every other action, and they happen during time away too, once the list is on your phone (it is from the first moment he is in that country that year).

Countries Nager.Date doesn't cover simply have no holidays. If it can't be reached, the game tries again next time you open it.

The first time he reaches a landmark he gets its card. Landmarks are places of these kinds: {LANDMARK_KINDS}. Art shops don't count.

On top of its rarity a card can have a finish: {GOLD_PCT} of cards are Gold and {FOIL_PCT} are Foil. Each landmark has one card, given on his first visit; going back gives nothing more, and a card's finish never changes. The feed says "New card", and the Collection sheet shows them all.
## City cards {citycards}
City cards are trading cards of the world's cities: the {CITY_CARDS_PER_COUNTRY} biggest cities of every country, {CITY_CARD_TOTAL} cards in all. A card's rarity is fixed by how big its city is in its country:
- the biggest: Rare,
- the next {UNCOMMON_CITY_COUNT}: Uncommon,
- the rest: Common.

Cards come in packs of {PACK_SIZE}. He gets a free pack each time he levels up, and for each new city and each new country he reaches, and opens them at his next stop. He also buys packs at {CARD_SHOPS}: "Buy a city card pack" costs {PACK_PRICE}. He sells his spares the next time he reaches one of those shops: {SPARE_COMMON} for a Common, {SPARE_UNCOMMON} for an Uncommon and {SPARE_RARE} for a Rare, {FOIL_MULT} times that if Foil and {GOLD_MULT} times if Gold.

When he has every card of a country, its set is done: he gets Charm +{SET_CHARM} while he is in that country. At made-up places (see While you were away) he doesn't sell spares or open free packs; those wait for his next real stop.
## Placedex {placedex}
The Placedex counts every kind of place he has been to, such as a cafe, a Christian place of worship or a viewpoint. The first time he reaches a kind he has never seen, he gets bonus XP by how rare that kind is:
- Common: +{DEX_XP_COMMON} XP,
- Uncommon: +{DEX_XP_UNCOMMON} XP,
- Rare: +{DEX_XP_RARE} XP,
- Legendary: +{DEX_XP_LEGENDARY} XP.

Rarity comes from a POI sweep of big cities around the world: a kind found {DEX_COMMON_MIN} or more times there is Common, {DEX_UNCOMMON_MIN} or more Uncommon, {DEX_RARE_MIN} or more Rare, and anything rarer, or never found at all, is Legendary. It knows {DEX_KINDS} kinds, bridges among them: the game takes bridges from the streets rather than the sweep and counts them as Common. This XP comes on top of the first-visit XP and isn't changed by buffs or mood.
## Souvenirs {souvenirs}
Every country has one souvenir for him, and it costs nothing. Each time he reaches a landmark (see Landmark cards) in a country he has no souvenir from, there is a {SOUVENIR_PCT} chance he finds it there. {SOUVENIR_COUNTRIES} countries have their own souvenir, such as a bottle of rose oil from Bulgaria; anywhere else it is a fridge magnet.

Souvenirs make him a seasoned traveller: every {SOUVENIRS_PER_CUT} souvenirs make his fares {FARE_CUT_PCT} cheaper, up to {FARE_CUT_MAX_PCT} (see Fares).

{GIFT_LIST}

## His trust in you {trust}
He doesn't know who you are, but he can tell someone is looking out for him. His trust in you runs from 0 to {TRUST_MAX} and starts at {TRUST_START}. The Care package sheet shows it.

## Whispers {whisper}
Up to {WHISPERS_PER_DAY} times a day (by your device's date) you can whisper one word to him from the Care package sheet. He listens with a chance of {HEED_MIN_PCT}, plus {HEED_PER_TRUST_PCT} for each point of trust, so {HEED_FULL_PCT} at full trust. Otherwise he shrugs it off. Either way his History says so. When he listens, he follows the whisper for {NUDGE_MIN} minutes:
- 🍞 Eat: he acts as if he is starving and thirsty: food and drink are all he looks for, the nearest first, and he won't start a new trip (see His needs).
- 💤 Rest: he acts as if he has no energy left: coffee, benches and naps are all he looks for, the nearest first, and he won't start a new trip.

Whispers are always optional, and they never use up clovers. His dice for whether he listens are separate from his walk's, so a whisper changes only what he would have done next.
## Kind or rascal {lean}

## Guess the next trip {guess}
When the trip dice send him to another city, the top card shows "🎲 Where to?" with up to {GUESS_CHOICES} choices while he walks to the station:
- for a trip in his country, the right city and other cities of the country,
- for a trip abroad, the right country and other countries.

The city he is in is never a choice. When the bundled city list has no other city to offer, there are fewer choices, or no guess for that trip.

Tap one to guess. You get one guess per trip. When he boards, his History says whether you were right, and a right guess earns {GUESS_CLOVERS} ☘ clover (see Clovers and care packages), on top of the daily ones. If you don't guess, nothing changes: he goes all the same.

If the trip is called off before he boards (he can't pay, or he faints), the guess goes with it. So does leaving the game for {MIN_AWAY_MIN} minute or more before he boards: the catch-up starts him afresh at a place (see While you were away). Trips he takes while you are away have no guess.

Opening a postcard changes nothing in their game.
## One choice on reopen {dilemma}
Now and then, when you open the game after at least {DILEMMA_AWAY_H} hours away ({DILEMMA_PCT} of such reopens), the While you were away card holds one small choice, such as "A trucker offers a free ride to Varna." Tap an option and it happens. Or just carry on: when the card closes, or you leave the game with it open, he decides by himself, and his History says "Decided by himself". Nothing waits for you.

When he decides, it's even odds, tilted toward the first option by {LEAN_PCT} for each point of the attribute he leans on above 1, up to {LEAN_CAP_PCT}. Below 1 it stays even. The choices:
{DILEMMA_LIST}

At chess he wins {CHESS_BASE_PCT} of the time, plus {CHESS_PER_WIT_PCT} for each point of Wits, up to {CHESS_MAX_PCT}.

A free ride takes him there at once and at no cost, unlike a normal trip (see Trips to other cities). There are no free rides while he is on a paid one; if he boards one while the card is open, he turns the free ride down himself. A free ride replaces a trip he is still walking to the station for, and its guess.
# 📖 Stories
## The storyteller {storyteller}
A storyteller decides when something happens to him, the way RimWorld's do. It keeps a tension number from 0 to {TENSION_MAX}. Every hour of his life adds to it, faster for a wilder storyteller: {STORY_RATES} an hour. Money draws trouble: each {WEALTH_STEP} he has adds 1 more an hour, up to {WEALTH_CAP}.

Below {FIRE_AT} nothing happens. Above it, each place he reaches may bring an incident (see Incidents), likelier the higher the tension: none at {FIRE_AT}, {FIRE_MAX_PCT} of stops at {TENSION_MAX}.

An incident is good, bad or odd. The share of bad ones depends on the storyteller: {STORY_BAD}. Luck makes bad ones rarer, the same way it makes mishaps rarer (see Luck). After a bad one the tension drops to 0, and for {LUCKY_H} hours only good or odd things happen. A good or odd one leaves {KEEP_AFTER_GOOD_PCT} of the tension. The last {RECENT_INCIDENTS} incidents don't come again while another one fits.

Pick the storyteller in ☰ Menu → Storyteller: each tap switches between Calm, Classic and Chaotic. It starts on Classic, is kept with his game, and counts from his next stop. It runs while you are away too.
## Incidents {incidents}
Incidents are things that happen to him, not things he chooses to do: a stray dog follows him, a wedding waves him in for cake, a pickpocket brushes past. The storyteller decides when (see The storyteller). There are {INCIDENT_COUNT}: {GOOD_INCIDENTS} good, {BAD_INCIDENTS} bad and {ODD_INCIDENTS} odd.

Some only happen at some places (a wedding at a church or town hall, food poisoning at a restaurant or snack bar), at some times (quiz night at a pub in the evening), or while he gets about a certain way (a bike thief only when he rides a bike, a parking ticket only by car or motorbike, and a few only on a fantasy mount).

Each one is a line in his History that starts with its icon, and the line over the map stands out: bold, on a shaded card with an orange edge.

A bad one never takes more than {LOSS_CAP_PCT} of his money. It may take one item from his bag, but never his gear or a treasured item. A high enough attribute wards some off:
{GUARD_LIST}

{CHOICE_INCIDENTS} incidents hold a choice of two, such as a street musician asking him to sing along. He picks by himself, at even odds tilted toward the bolder option by {INC_LEAN_PCT} for each point of its attribute above 1, up to {INC_LEAN_CAP_PCT}. Nothing waits for you.
## Quests {quests}
Quests are the heart of his life. Every city has a board of them, rolled the first time {BOARD_PLACES} of its places (not counting bridges) are loaded, with at least one where a quest can start: {BOARD_MIN} in a town of {BOARD_TOWN_POP} people or fewer, {BOARD_MAX} in a city of {BOARD_BIG_POP} or more, and in between on a sliding scale (about {BOARD_MID} in a city of {BOARD_MID_POP}). There are {QUEST_KINDS} kinds of quest, {FANTASY_KINDS} of them with a touch of magic (at most {FANTASY_PCT} of a board), and each one is filled with a local's name, an item, sometimes a twist and the city's real places, so the same kind reads as a new quest: over {QUEST_VARIANTS} in all. A quest he has done never comes back, so when he returns to a city it has new ones.

{CITY_CITIES} cities, nearly every capital among them, also have quests of their own, {CITY_QUESTS} in all, about their real landmarks: they go on that city's board before any other, and he does each one once. Where the landmark isn't loaded yet, a place of the same kind stands in.

After every {QUEST_EVERY_MIN} to {QUEST_EVERY_MAX} stops he takes one on, likelier one near him, and does its steps back to back (a step at a place closed for the night waits for morning, see Opening hours): first the quest giver, then {STEPS_MORE_MIN} to {STEPS_MORE_MAX} more places of the right kinds (a pharmacy, then the neighbour's café), each picked among the {STEP_CHOICES} nearest that fit. Each step takes {STEP_MIN} minutes instead of an action. An urgent need (see His needs) comes first: he eats or sleeps, then carries on. He doesn't set off on a trip while he is on a quest; if a free ride takes him away anyway, the quest stays behind, along with the city's board and events.

At its middle step, where a twist may come, it goes on at even odds, tilted by {INC_LEAN_PCT} for each point of the quest's attribute above 1 (up to {INC_LEAN_CAP_PCT}). Otherwise it fails there and ends early with no reward, and it doesn't come back either; the Quests card counts only those he finished. One that goes on pays its money, XP and effects at the end, now and then with a thank-you item for his bag. Nothing waits for you.

On the map, a purple ! marks each quest on the board, at its giver's place, and a purple flag the step he is on, with a yellow glow. Tap one to read it; Go help sends him to that quest next, without waiting for the stops in between (once its place is open, see Opening hours). It is only a nudge: he does just as well without it. The ❗ Quests card on the Stats sheet shows what he is on and his last {CARD_RECENT}. The {LOG_MAX} newest quests and events are kept.

While you are away he takes on quests, does their steps and joins events the same way, in a city whose places are loaded.
## World events {world_events}
World events happen at real places near him whether he is there or not, like Guild Wars 2's events: an oven breaks down at a bakery, a parrot escapes from a pet shop, a tour group loses its guide. At each stop that isn't a quest step or an event there is a {EVENT_CHANCE_PCT} chance of a new one within {EVENT_NEAR_KM} km, while fewer than {EVENT_MAX} run; each lasts the minutes it says, and some only happen in the morning, by day, in the evening or at night. There are {EVENT_KINDS}, {FOLLOW_KINDS} of them follow-ups. {EVENT_FANTASY} of them have a touch of magic, such as a statue that steps down at night or a pigeon that asks the time.

A purple star marks each one on the map. When he picks where to go next and isn't setting off on a trip, one within {EVENT_NEAR_KM} km comes before anything else unless a need is urgent or its place is closed (see Opening hours; tap one and Go help to send him to that one first, however far). Helping takes {STEP_MIN} minutes and goes well at the same odds as a quest, with its own attribute. Win or lose, a follow-up may come next ({FOLLOW_PCT} of the time when there is one): a stalled museum move leaves boxes to sort, a lost child becomes a big search. An event he misses plays out without him, and word of it reaches his History.
## Night legends {legends}
At night ({TIME_NIGHT}) at castles, graveyards, memorials, shrines, old wells, wizard towers and the like, the storyteller may tell a night legend instead: a pale lady on a castle wall, a lantern between the graves, a black cat that only greets the lucky. There are {LEGEND_COUNT}, each written for its kind of place. They come like any incident (see The storyteller), but where one fits it is {LEGEND_WEIGHT} times likelier than any other card.
## Viktor, his rival {rival}
{RIVAL_ICON} {RIVAL_NAME} is another traveller. When he reaches a new city, {RIVAL_TOWN_PCT} of the time Viktor is there too. Then each place he reaches has a {RIVAL_MEET_PCT} chance of a meeting, when no incident happens there and it isn't his club night or an invitation to a club (see Clubs). It all happens by itself.

Most meetings are a contest for the same thing, at a place where it fits: the same day's work, the last window table, a chess game, a pub quiz, the last croissant, a photo spot, an arm-wrestle, a race to the corner. Each leans on an attribute. His odds are his attribute plus {HIS_PER_LEVEL} per level of his, against {RIVAL_PER_LEVEL} per level of Viktor's (Viktor starts at level {RIVAL_START_LEVEL}). A win gives {WIN_FX}; a loss {LOSE_FX}, and Viktor goes up a level.

Some meetings are a friendly chat instead: {FRIENDLY_PCT} of them, plus {FRIENDLY_PER_CHA_PCT} per point of Charm above 1, up to {FRIENDLY_CAP_PCT}, and always where no contest fits. A chat gives {FRIENDLY_FX}.

Viktor remembers. He brings up where they last met in another city, and every contest makes his feelings {FEELING_STEP} colder while every chat makes them {FEELING_STEP} warmer (from -{FEELING_MAX} to {FEELING_MAX}). At -{FEELING_AT} he is a Nemesis; at {FEELING_AT} a Friendly rival.

For {SEEN_MIN} minutes after a meeting he shows on the map beside him, as a smaller round {RIVAL_ICON} on a cream badge. His card on the Stats sheet keeps the score.
## Friends and a partner {friends}
He makes friends by himself. At a kind of place where he knows no one who hangs out there, each stop has a {FRIEND_NEW_PCT} chance he gets talking with a local, who then hangs out at that kind of place in that city. Back at such a place, a stop has a {FRIEND_SEE_PCT} chance he runs into one of them again, and each meeting adds a heart 💗, up to {HEARTS_MAX}. Friends only come at a stop where no incident happens, no club night or club invitation comes (see Clubs), and Viktor doesn't turn up.

From {MEET_UP_AT} hearts a friend also meets up with him on purpose anywhere in their city: a {MEET_UP_PCT} chance at each stop. He keeps up to {FRIENDS_PER_CITY} friends in each city; a new one takes the place of the one with the fewest hearts.

The first friend to reach {HEARTS_MAX} hearts becomes his partner, for good. His partner comes along on every trip, and is never forgotten to make room. The Stats sheet lists his friends with their hearts.
## Travel companions {companions}
Until he has a partner, a fellow traveller joins him on {COMPANION_PCT} of his trips: a student, a retired sailor, a backpacker and others. They split the fare he paid ({SPLIT_PCT} of it back to him, never of a fine) and chat at his next {COMPANION_STOPS} quiet stops (no incident, club night or invitation, or Viktor), then go their own way at the one after. While one is along, the traveller takes those stops: no friends turn up, and no second traveller joins.
## Clubs {clubs}
A friend with {CLUB_JOIN_AT} hearts or more may invite him to their club: a {CLUB_INVITE_PCT} chance at each quiet stop (no incident) in their city, while he has no club there. The club meets at the place where he and the friend met (only a friend still living in that city invites him), on one weekday at its own hour, by your phone's clock. He belongs to one club a city and {CLUB_MAX} in all.

From then on he goes by himself: when he is in that city on that day, the first quiet stop (no incident) anywhere in the city in the {CLUB_NIGHT_H} hours from the club's start becomes the club night, as he heads over, and the friend gets a heart. One night a week, never more. If he is somewhere else, the club meets without him. The Stats sheet lists his clubs on the Friends card.
{CLUB_LIST}
The hedge mages' circle is the one club not of this world: it meets at a wizard's tower (see Made-up places).
## Friends' news {townies}
His friends live their own lives, also while you are away. Each day there is a {NEWS_PCT} chance that one of them with {NEWS_AT} hearts or more has news: a new hobby, a dog, a wedding, a baby, a shop of their own. His partner is with him, so has none. Big news comes as a letter to him, marked 💌 (his own letters to you are ✉); the rest is a line in the feed. Each piece of news happens to a friend once.

A friend may move to another city he has been to. From then on that is their city: he may run into them there, at the same kind of place.
## Anniversaries {anniversaries}
The day he set out is his birthday (in a game begun before his life story was kept, the first day after anniversaries came to the game). Each year on it he has a party in the city he is in, with his partner and the friends there with {PARTY_AT} hearts or more ({PARTY_MAX} at most), for {PARTY_MOOD} mood. With his mood out of 100 as the chance (at least {GREAT_MIN_PCT}), it is a great one, and he glows for {GLOW_H} hours: {GLOW_PER_H} mood an hour. With no friends there it is a quiet one alone, {ALONE_MOOD} mood.

On the same day of each of the other months (or the month's last day, when it is shorter) he marks the time on the road, such as "3 months on the road", for {MONTH_MOOD} mood. Both happen at the first minute of the day, also while you are away.
## His pet {pet}
When a stray dog or cat follows him (see Incidents) and he has no pet, {ADOPT_PCT} of the time it stays with him for good. He names it himself. On the map it trots at his side, opposite any fantasy mount.

Each day, at the first minute of the day (also while you are away), he buys it {PET_FOOD} of food. Fed, it lifts his mood by {PET_MOOD_PER_H} an hour, plus {PET_MOOD_PER_LEVEL} for each level above 1. If he can't pay, it is grumpy that day and gives no lift. It never runs off.

It grows a level with the km it walks at his side, on foot or by bike; on other rides it just comes along. Level 2 takes {PET_KM_2} km, level 3 {PET_KM_3} km, and so on, up to level {PET_MAX_LEVEL} at {PET_KM_MAX} km. Each level makes its perk bigger:
- 🐕 a dog sniffs out money on the street: {DOG_FIND_PCT} likelier a level (see Luck),
- 🐈 a cat keeps mishaps away: mishaps and bad incidents {CAT_RISK_PCT} rarer a level.

From level {PET_FAIRY_LEVEL}, at a real castle or old monument, {PET_FAIRY_PCT} of the time it shows what it really is: a dog was a 🐺 cù sìth, a fairy hound, and a cat a 🐈‍⬛ cat sìth. From then on its perk counts {PET_FAIRY_MULT} times as much.

A pet lives with him for {PET_LIFE_MIN} to {PET_LIFE_MAX} days. Then it passes away in its sleep (a fairy one goes back to the hills), he misses it ({MISSING_MOOD_PER_H} mood an hour for {PET_MISSING_H} hours), and a new stray may stay. The Stats sheet shows his pet, and the ones he had under Remembered.
# 📱 Screens and buttons
## The top card and buttons {screen}
The card at the top shows the city and what he is doing (and how he feels, unless he is just Fine: see Emotions), his money and level, the weather and temperature in his city (the season's icon while the weather is unknown or he is on a trip to another city), the street he is on (or the ride he is on), his goal with the distance and the approximate walking time, the "🎲 Where to?" guess while he walks to a station for a trip, today's forecast in one dim line (see Daily forecast), and three small bars: ❤ health, ⚡ energy and 🍞 fullness. While he rests it shows the countdown and a bar. A red line in it warns about anything that could leave him stuck; tap it to open Debug.

What he just did pops up under it for a few seconds.

Only three buttons sit on the map, at the bottom right in reach of your thumb: + and − to zoom, and ☰ for the menu. The small ⓘ at the bottom left shows the map credits. "Back to him" shows up at the bottom when you have dragged the map away while the camera follows him.
## The menu {menu}
☰ Menu (bottom right) opens from the bottom of the screen and holds History, Stats, Collection, Care package (with your clovers), Send a postcard, Help, Suggest a line (see Suggest a line), Debug, the Route to goal, Camera follows him, City area and City sound switches, Storyteller (see The storyteller), and New city. Tap an entry to open it; ✕ Close or the Escape key closes it again.

To see more of the map, drag any sheet down by its title bar (the one with the grip line). It stays at the height where you let go, up to {SHEET_TALL_PCT}% of the screen (all of it for full-screen sheets like Stats and Help). Let go below {SHEET_CLOSE_PCT}% of the screen and it closes. Drag it back up to see more of it.

Camera follows him (a switch, on at first and remembered) keeps him in view: the map glides over to him whenever he walks out of the middle of the screen. Turn it off and the map stays wherever you leave it, even when he walks off the screen (see The map).

Route to goal (a switch, off at first and remembered) draws his planned route in orange: a solid line along the streets he will take, then a dashed straight line for the last part where the streets are not loaded yet.

City sound (a switch, off at first and remembered) plays a soft loop for where he is (see City sound).

- Saving for: the vehicle he saves up for (see Saving for a vehicle) and any trip fare, each with a gold bar of how much he has.
- Vitals: a coloured bar and the value for each of his six meters, and an indigo one for Magic (see Magic), with what his enchanted gear adds a km.

- Mind: his wants and fears, and a Mood list of everything that moves his mood each hour (see Moodlets and Wants and fears).

## The Collection sheet {collection}
🗂 Collection (in the menu) shows everything he collects. You never have to open it: he collects on his own.

At the top, Completion counts every tile he has found and every badge tier he holds, out of all of them. Under it are his badges and his title (see Badges and titles). Then come five tabs:
- 🎴 Cards: landmark cards (see Landmark cards), newest first (the newest {CARDS_SHOWN}), and city cards with a row per country he has cards from.
- 📇 Places: the Placedex by rarity, the odd places the game makes up (see Made-up places), the creatures he has met (see Place creatures), the giants he has seen (see Giants seen from afar), his fairy gifts and stories (see Fairy rings) and the crawls.

A Gold badge also gives him a title, shown on the Collection and Stats sheets: the newest one he earned. Badges and titles give nothing else.

## History {history}
📜 History lists his last {HISTORY_SIZE} events, newest first, each with the time it happened: arrivals, actions, purchases, level-ups, trips and faints. That includes what he did while you were away, at the times it happened.

While it is open, the map shows the trail he walked (up to about {TRAIL_KM} km), with numbered markers where things happened. Events at the same spot share one marker, such as "4-6". ## Debug {debug}
Debug (in the menu) is for testing and curious players.

Severe problems are ones that could leave him stuck. Once the streets all around him have loaded, the game checks the city box for a way out (a bus station, an airport or a gas station) and for a place that pays him. Missing ones are normally made up (see Made-up places), so this only shows when that fails, for example when the game is offline before it has kept that part of its code. If one is missing, a red ⛔ line stays in the top card until the city has it or he leaves. A city without a gas station is flagged too.

At the top of Debug, coloured boxes list what looks wrong with the city right now. Cards below show the city, what has loaded (street squares, places, trivia), the ways out and places that pay, and the trip dice.

Then come three logs, closed at first: the city's trivia, Problems (the last {MAX_SEVERE} severe problems, {MAX_ERRORS} errors and {MAX_WARNINGS} warnings from anywhere in the game) and Goal picks (his last {DEBUG_SIZE} goals). Each entry is a short title, such as "🎯 Chosen: Cafe East". Tap it to see how it happened: every dice roll, the odds and why. A random spot counts as a warning, unless it is only because every place near enough is closed. A red dot on ☰ and Debug means there are problems you haven't looked at yet. Problems are kept even through New city.

City area (a switch in the menu) shades the box his goals are picked from in orange and draws the city's real border from OpenStreetMap in purple, so you can see where the two differ. The switch is remembered.

Its buttons change the game right away:
- New goal ends any stop or trip and picks a new goal now, rolling the trip dice again.
- New city sends him straight to another city in the same country.
- New country sends him straight to a city in another country.

The 🤔 Dilemmas switch, "One on every long reopen", makes every reopen after {DILEMMA_AWAY_H} hours bring a choice (see One choice on reopen), for testing. The switch is remembered.
## New city {new_city}
New city (in the menu) starts the whole game over in a city you choose. Because it wipes everything (stats, money, gear, bag, History and the goal-pick log), you have to type RESET in capitals and confirm.

To just see him somewhere else without losing anything, wait for a trip, or use the Debug buttons.
## About Help {help}
Help (in the menu) explains every part of the game on its own page. Tap a topic to open it and Back to return to the list.

To find something fast, type in the search box at the top. Results appear as you type: every page that holds all the words you typed, in any order, with pages whose title matches first. Words are matched from their start ("fast" finds "faster", but "fee" doesn't find "coffee"), and capitals and accents don't matter ("cafe" finds "Café"). Each result shows the line where your word was found, with the word marked. Tap one to open the page; Back brings you to your results, and ✖ clears the box and shows the full list again.

The numbers on these pages come straight from the game, so they are always the ones it really uses. Help is updated whenever a mechanic changes.
